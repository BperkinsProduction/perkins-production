Farm to glass, all of it here

Grapes, lavender, corn and hay grow on this ground. The water comes up through limestone under your feet. Everything in your glass was made within sight of where you are sitting.

Springfield Manor takes its name from the Limestone Spring Water rising through the aquifer beneath the estate. Ten wines, eleven beers and a shelf of award winning spirits are produced on site, poured in a renovated barn with patio seating, indoor tasting bars and live music. There is nowhere else in Maryland where all three happen on the same farm.

Two blooms a year, not one

The September field almost nobody knows about

The English varieties bloom twice. The main bloom lands in mid June, which is when the festival runs and when 2,600 plants turn the hillside purple all at once. Then, quietly, they do it again in September.

The second bloom is less profuse, and many visitors prefer it for exactly that reason: cooler air, thinner crowds, and the whole field to yourself. It is the best kept secret on the farm.

On the finished site, the banner at the top of every page knows which bloom is happening and says so. In June it sells festival tickets. In September it invites people to walk a quiet field. Nobody has to remember to change it twice.

Now imagine it filmed

A farm this photogenic should not be selling itself in text

Every image panel on this page is deliberately empty. No stock photography, ever. Your lavender rows, your copper still, your barn with the lights on and a band playing, your fainting goats wandering past the tasting bar, all photographed and filmed here by Perkins Production.

The finished site opens on that footage: a drone lifting off the purple rows at dusk, gin coming off the still, a bride in the vineyard at golden hour. Three seconds of that outsells three paragraphs.

Photography and video are not an add on here. They are the entire product.
